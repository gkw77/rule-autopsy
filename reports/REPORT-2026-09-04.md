# REPORT — 2026-09-04

Self-autopsy of a real 7-stage agent rules system, using its own honesty rule.

The rulebase this reports on (210 markdown blocks across `common/` + `python/` staging)
is private. Only **aggregate numbers** are published here. The detector is public
(`verify-claims/`) and reproduces this classification on any rules directory.

## Headline

> **Of 50 rule blocks that claim a measurable effect, 43 (86%) carry no proof the claim
> was ever measured.** The system's own rule — *a rule that isn't measured is faith* —
> convicts 86% of its own effect-claims.

## 3. Manual-triage caveat on the "selftested" count

The 7 "selftested" labels are detector flags, not proof. Manual triage (the backstop the
detector's own header demands) shows at least 2 are the audit tool describing *itself*
(A4 self-reference) — misflagged as self-measurement. The genuinely independent, committed
rigs behind the system number **2** (see `../receipts/`: gzh dual-gate, dao-cache), plus 1
reproduction (agent-chief) and 2 premise measurements (mcp-audit, post-verify-edit).

## 5. Detector sensitivity (honesty note)

The headline ratio depends on how strictly "claims an effect" is detected. A narrower
claim-regex on the same corpus flags **24** effect-claim blocks (79% unproven); the shipped
detector flags **50** (86% unproven). Both directions agree on the qualitative result: the
large majority of effect claims carry no self-measurement. Report a range in any citation.

## 6. What this does NOT claim

- It does not claim the other 160 blocks are bad — methodology prose is the point of a rules
  system, and A4 only demands receipts for rules that claim a measurable effect.
- It does not claim any specific rule is *wrong* or dead weight. Measuring catch-rate and
  false-block-rate per rule — the "which of my rules actually earn their tokens" experiment —
  is the roadmap (incident-regression harness), not this report.
